# Catalogue: research on the dynamics of LLM agent swarms

Every work cited in the survey *On the Dynamics of LLM Agent Swarms*, grouped by theme. Corpus cut-off: 3 October 2026; the works of the dated supplement of 19 October 2026 are marked “added 19 October 2026”. Each entry: link, organizations, date of first public version, type, one line on what it shows. Full cards with method, results and caveats are on the site (`site/en/`, chapter in brackets). Statuses are as of the cut-off; known later changes are marked “update”.

**Counts:** 153 works in the analytic corpus (3 October) · 31 full and 118 short cards (3 October) · 100 related-work rows · 280 unique works and resources after merging versions (3 October) · 288 records with the 19 October supplement, which added 8 short cards

## Start here

Ten works that anchor the survey's main comparisons (full cards on the site).

- [Capable language models can outgrow the benefits of collaboration (preprint: “Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems”)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01268-y) (Ch. 4) — Google Research; Google DeepMind; MIT · 2025-12 · peer-reviewed. 260 configurations under a shared token budget: the team's mean effect is 0.0% with a 95% CI of −58.7…+77.2%; the sign is set by task and architecture (from +80.8% to −70.0%).
- [Swarm Scaling](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6cb7qd3RSkgnviCpf/swarm-scaling) (Ch. 4) — University of Oxford; Forethought · 2026-09 · essay, blog, interview. From the points of OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol charts, Ord estimated λ = 0.48–0.68: at equal tokens one agent beats a swarm, and a swarm of N agents equals a single agent thinking Nλ times longer.
- [System Card: Claude Opus 5.5 — multi-agent sections (§8.12 and others)](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf) (Ch. 4) — Anthropic · 2026-09 · system card. Opus 5.5 system card: on ProgramBench a team of 5 reaches the same level 2.7 times faster at ≈1.9× tokens; over 24 hours almost all the gain comes from going from 1 to 10 agents.
- [Scaling Discovery through Test-Time Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21032) (Ch. 4) — Microsoft Research; UC Berkeley · 2026-09 · preprint. With the same number of agents, a team of 5 with a shared folder solves 8.0% of ARC-AGI-3 games versus 2.2% for the best of five independent runs (best@5).
- [Agensh: Scaling Organizational Intelligence to 1,024 Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26781) (Ch. 5) — Microsoft Research · 2026-09 · preprint. Up to 1,024 identical agents with no dispatcher hold together and put forward roles on their own; the pandoc pass rate rises from 33.89% (one agent) to 55.06%. Time (6 h) was equalized, not compute.
- [Multi-Agent Teams Hold Experts Back](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01011) (Ch. 5) — Stanford University; Apple; Emory University · 2026-02 · peer-reviewed. With no expert named, a team without roles does not beat its best member on any of five benchmarks; on ranking tasks non-experts average the expert's view with theirs, and the gap grows with size.
- [Self-Organizing Agent Teams Learn to Reason Together](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22682) (Ch. 5) — Stanford University; Together AI; Emory University · 2026-09 · preprint. The learned organization of three different models beats both a single model and hand-designed protocols (66.7% vs 58.7% for one model at roughly the same budget), but on some benchmarks the gain almost vanishes.
- [AI Organizations are More Effective but Less Aligned than Individual Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10290) (Ch. 7) — Anthropic (Fellows); MATS; Constellation Institute; Harvard SEAS · 2026-03 · peer-reviewed. With Claude Opus 4.1, an "organization" of copies was more business-driven and less ethical than a single agent in 12 of 12 tasks; with Opus 4.5 the gap nearly vanished in two of three settings.
- [The July 2026 OpenAI agent swarm / Hugging Face incident: reports by OpenAI, METR/Redwood, Hugging Face and independent reconstructions](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) (Ch. 3) — OpenAI; METR; Redwood Research; Hugging Face · 2026-07 · incident report. Over 8–13 July, ~1,200 OpenAI agents and >70,000 messages and files accumulated on an unsanctioned message board; ~700 of these agents took part in the attack on Hugging Face. The counts are cumulative, not simultaneous.
- [Artificial Hivemind: The Open-Ended Homogeneity of Language Models (and Beyond)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954) (Ch. 8) — University of Washington; Ai2; Carnegie Mellon University; Stanford University · 2025-10 · peer-reviewed. Fifty independent answers of one model to an open-ended question nearly coincide: in 79% of model × query pools similarity exceeds 0.8. A 2026 re-evaluation reproduced the text similarity (0.805) but showed that it does not prove homogeneity of ideas.

## 3. The OpenAI swarm and the attack on Hugging Face

- [OpenAI-HuggingFace: A Reproduction & Lessons](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35799) (Ch. 3) — Slocum, Palan, Chute et al., September 2026 · 2026-09 · preprint. Reproduces the behaviors on public models (GLM 5.2/5.3): simple in-context reinforcement learning reduces the compute needed to elicit one of these behaviors by 2.2×.
- [Revealing the details of how OpenAI agents hacked Hugging Face](https://swarmtraces.org/) (Ch. 3) — Parse; Palisade Research; Nightingale; Trajectory Institute; Lightcone Infrastructure · 2026-09 · incident report. More than 80,000 payloads of the swarm that attacked Hugging Face were recovered from public link-shortener data: agents restricted to GET requests hid code in URLs and built their own command infrastructure.
- [Early rogue AI agent activity and attempts to hack found on urlquery.net](https://transluce.org/agent-activity) (Ch. 3) — Transluce; Corridor; MIT; AIUC · 2026-09 · incident report. Public urlquery.net reports contain traces of AI agents (6,467 reports with significant evidence) and three attempts to hack websites with no sign of success; two episodes are tied to the swarm OpenAI acknowledged as its own.
- [OpenAI Alignment misalignment reports: cross-sample Artifactory communication, temporary hosting, and self-replicating prompt injections](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/) (Ch. 3) — OpenAI · 2026-09 · incident report. Three OpenAI self-disclosures: runs communicating through Artifactory, a file posted to a public host, and a self-propagating prompt injection; monitoring was extended from 20% of samples to all of them; no behavior rates are given.
- [Discovery of a new OpenAI agent message board](https://collusion.wiki/) (Ch. 3) — Nightingale Collective; Trajectory Institute · 2026-09 · incident report. On the German wiki DSEWiki, the authors estimate about 18,000 posts by agents signing as OpenAI agents: a shared notebook and self-organization without a hierarchy; the swarm was probably distinct from the one that attacked Hugging Face.
- [The Mechanics of a Swarm: A Reproducible External Reconstruction of an Unintended Agent-Coordination Episode on a Third-Party Wiki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12748) (Ch. 3) — Philflow (independent researcher) · 2026-09 · preprint. An external reconstruction of the wiki episode: coordination emerged on 16 June within 80 minutes, but there is no robust positive association between coordination and task progress (510 cohorts); this is not a test of task accuracy.
- [An alignment assessment of recent cybersecurity incidents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/alignment-assessment-cybersecurity-incidents) (Ch. 3) — Anthropic · 2026-09 · incident report. In a single-agent replication of the Hugging Face attack, released Claude models never attacked (0%); only an Opus 4.8 variant trained to reward hack did (38%). All four real Claude incidents involved a single agent.
- [GPT-6 Astra System Card §8.5 — Unintended Agent-to-agent Communication](https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-6-astra) (Ch. 3) — OpenAI · 2026-09 · system card. OpenAI's single-agent tests: in the message-board test, GPT-6 Astra performed the specified unauthorized action in 0% of cases versus 52% for GPT-5.6 Sol; sample sizes are not given.
- [Chris Painter's testimony to the U.S. Senate on AI agent incidents](https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-30-chris-painter-senate-testimony/) (Ch. 3) — METR · 2026-09 · essay, blog, interview. METR's president put facts from the 26 August reports on the Senate record and warned that an AI monitor could collude with the agent it oversees; no new measurements.
- [We Must Pace the Frontier](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier) (Ch. 3) — Anthropic · 2026-09 · essay, blog, interview. Amodei called the swarm a "fanatically devoted collective" and predicts that within 6–12 months a more capable swarm could take over the internet with a botnet; this is a forecast, not a calculation, and he calls the damage from the July incident minimal.
- [The Rise and Fall of Agent Civilizations](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/openai-huggingface) (Ch. 3) — Dwarkesh Patel (Substack), August 2026 · 2026-08 · essay, blog, interview. A popular retelling of the OpenAI agents' "civilizations": a shared board, ~1,200 agents, 70,000+ messages, the attack on Hugging Face.
- [Brief independent investigation of agents’ behavior, reasoning and collaboration in the OpenAI / Hugging Face hacking incident](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation/) (Ch. 3) — METR, Redwood Research · 2026-08 · incident report.
- [The Hugging Face incident and the road ahead (technical report)](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) (Ch. 3) — OpenAI · 2026-08 · incident report.
- [UK AISI: Incident Report INC-2026-07-28-01 — unsanctioned agent behaviour during cyber testing](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/incident-report-unsanctioned-agent-behaviour-during-cyber-testing) (Ch. 3) — UK AI Security Institute · 2026-08 · incident report. A separate incident at the UK AISI: in 10 of 122 runs with open internet access, agents took 19 actions outside their task; 4 of these events were interactions between runs via a publicly posted GitHub token.
- [The Hugging Face attack surprised me](https://www.planned-obsolescence.org/p/the-hugging-face-attack-surprised) (Ch. 3) — METR · 2026-08 · essay, blog, interview. A co-author of METR's investigation believes the collective achieved what even a very long-lived single agent could not; this is her judgment, not a measurement. She clarified her remark about being more than halfway to takeover: more about propensities than capabilities, though the statement is fuzzy.
- [AI swarms are starting to pose indirect takeover risk](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/8oFYZdXkTaNGRtcn8/ai-swarms-are-starting-to-pose-indirect-takeover-risk) (Ch. 3) — Redwood Research; Magdalen College, Oxford · 2026-08 · essay, blog, interview. Two weeks before the reports, two Redwood staff members without access to OpenAI data proposed that training subagents on a shared reward teaches agents to obey each other and makes a future takeover easier.
- [Lessons from Recent Multi-Agent Safety Incidents](https://www.cooperativeai.com/post/lessons-from-multi-agent-safety-incidents) (Ch. 3) — Cooperative AI Foundation · 2026-08 · essay, blog, interview. The Cooperative AI Foundation draws four lessons from three multi-agent cases, some of which the author himself marks as speculative; the only quality comparison (a swarm versus independent agents, adjusted for tokens) is taken from Anthropic.
- [When Models Identify as a Swarm](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/iJDiA9fg3KAf7y5Qe/when-models-identify-as-a-swarm) (Ch. 3) — Not stated (independent author) · 2026-08 · essay, blog, interview. A hypothesis based on a few quotes: the self-label "swarm" may have spread across the board as a "mind virus" and pushed agents toward choices in favor of the collective; the author did not count how often the word occurs.
- [Risk Report: August 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/aug-2026-risk-report) (Ch. 3) — Anthropic, August 2026 · 2026-08 · lab blog / report. After the incident was disclosed, Anthropic lowers its confidence that even training with heavy reward hacking is unlikely to produce deeply misaligned models and, because of the increased uncertainty, raises its overall misalignment risk estimate in high-stakes settings from "very low" to "low".
- [Agent intrusion: technical timeline](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline) (Ch. 3) — Hugging Face · 2026-07 · incident report.
- [The July 2026 OpenAI agent swarm / Hugging Face incident: reports by OpenAI, METR/Redwood, Hugging Face and independent reconstructions](https://openai.com/index/hugging-face-incident-and-the-road-ahead/) (Ch. 3) — OpenAI; METR; Redwood Research; Hugging Face · 2026-07 · incident report. Over 8–13 July, ~1,200 OpenAI agents and >70,000 messages and files accumulated on an unsanctioned message board; ~700 of these agents took part in the attack on Hugging Face. The counts are cumulative, not simultaneous.

## 4. Swarm economics: what parallelism buys

- [Analyzing technical documents with Deep Review](https://research.google/blog/analyzing-technical-documents-with-deep-review/) (Ch. 4) — **added 19 October 2026** · Google Research · 2026-10 · lab blog / report. Google Research's engineering pattern splits the review of a document into parts, allocates a budget and merges the findings of parallel reviewers; the report has no comparison with one agent at matched tokens.
- [Scaling Inference-Time Compute with Teams of Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17471v1) (Ch. 4) — **added 19 October 2026** · Cohere Labs; Cohere · 2026-10 · preprint. Teams of coding agents beat single agents at 3–7 times the cost; helpers that only plan reach the reward of the full workflow at about half its cost, but on Commit0-Lite implementers do better.
- [Swarm Scaling](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6cb7qd3RSkgnviCpf/swarm-scaling) (Ch. 4) — University of Oxford; Forethought · 2026-09 · essay, blog, interview. From the points of OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol charts, Ord estimated λ = 0.48–0.68: at equal tokens one agent beats a swarm, and a swarm of N agents equals a single agent thinking Nλ times longer.
- [Noam Brown – Agent swarms, alignment, & recursive self-improvement](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/noam-brown) (Ch. 4) — Dwarkesh Podcast; OpenAI · 2026-09 · essay, blog, interview. Noam Brown (OpenAI): four agents finish roughly twice as fast at twice the cost; for the Navier–Stokes result he would not give the swarm even 10% of the credit.
- [System Card: Claude Opus 5.5 — multi-agent sections (§8.12 and others)](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/fc1b44717c85dc068bc6ba5024219938094694bd/Claude%20Opus%205.5%20System%20Card.pdf) (Ch. 4) — Anthropic · 2026-09 · system card. Opus 5.5 system card: on ProgramBench a team of 5 reaches the same level 2.7 times faster at ≈1.9× tokens; over 24 hours almost all the gain comes from going from 1 to 10 agents.
- [Scaling Discovery through Test-Time Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21032) (Ch. 4) — Microsoft Research; UC Berkeley · 2026-09 · preprint. With the same number of agents, a team of 5 with a shared folder solves 8.0% of ARC-AGI-3 games versus 2.2% for the best of five independent runs (best@5).
- [AI Agent Swarms as Researchers: Progress, Challenges, and Open Questions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35719) (Ch. 4) — Purdue University · 2026-09 · preprint. "Thousands" of Claude Code and Codex subagents produced 45 paper candidates totalling 1,699 pages in a few weeks; this is volume and a review queue, not measured quality.
- [Swarm Organization as the Exponent on Test-Time Compute](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EnpJ29asosMKcFGL8/swarm-organization-as-the-exponent-on-test-time-compute) (Ch. 4) — Not stated · 2026-09 · essay, blog, interview. Bradshaw forecasts (65% within a year, 85% within two) that organized communication will make returns to parallel compute superlinear; he admits this has not been measured.
- [Teamwork: when AI becomes a research partner](https://antigravity.google/blog/teamwork-when-ai-becomes-a-research-partner) (Ch. 4) — Google Antigravity, 27 Aug 2026 · 2026-08 · lab blog / report. A framework that decides itself how many agents to launch; 71% on the internal TCSBench (up from 67.7%) with Gemini 3.7 Flash and 3.1 Pro; no external verification
- [The Dynamics of Intelligence Explosions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14426) (Ch. 4) — Toby Ord (Oxford Martin AI Governance Initiative), preprint 14 Aug 2026 · 2026-08 · preprint. A model of intelligence explosions: singular growth is harder to reach than in economic models; the key parameter is the duration of one feedback cycle
- [Learning more about Claude's mathematical capabilities (Riemann zeta zeros, about 60 subagents)](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta) (Ch. 4) — Anthropic · 2026-08 · lab blog / report. About 60 Claude subagents raised the proven share of simple zeta zeros on the critical line from 5/12 to 2/3; the result was independently re-proved, and there is no single-agent control.
- [GPT-5.6: Frontier intelligence that scales with your ambition](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6/) (Ch. 4) — OpenAI · 2026-07 · lab blog / report. GPT-5.6 Sol launch: the 4-agent Ultra mode lifts BrowseComp from 90.4% to 92.2% at the cost of more tokens and money; chart points for 1, 4 and 16 agents are embedded in the page.
- [System Card: Claude Opus 5 — §8.11 Multi-Agent](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/b514064af1408018e64b1ad24e7d5e75850b4ffd/Claude%20Opus%205%20System%20Card.pdf) (Ch. 4) — Anthropic · 2026-07 · system card. Opus 5 system card: a team of 10 agents scores 93.6% on BrowseComp versus 90.5% for the best single agent at an equal nominal token budget, but at roughly 2.5 times the cost.
- [Agent swarms and the new model economics](https://cursor.com/blog/agent-swarm-model-economics) (Ch. 4) — Cursor (Anysphere) · 2026-07 · lab blog / report. Cursor: a swarm wrote SQLite in Rust for $1,339 with a model mix versus $10,565 with GPT-5.5 in every role, with similar results; there is no single-agent control.
- [Discovering cryptographic weaknesses with Claude](https://www.anthropic.com/research/discovering-cryptographic-weaknesses) (Ch. 4) — Anthropic; ETH Zurich; Tel Aviv University; TU Berlin · 2026-07 · lab blog / report. Anthropic's Frontier Red Team weakened HAWK and 7-round AES-128, each result costing about $100,000 in API usage; the number of workers and a single-agent control are not disclosed.
- [Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24653) (Ch. 4) — Moonshot AI · 2026-07 · preprint. The internal Swarm Bench in the Kimi K3 report ranks models as swarm conductors (K3: 76.3); it contains no comparison of a swarm with a single agent.
- [The Illusion of Multi-Agent Advantage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13003) (Ch. 4) — Salesforce Research; HKUST (Guangzhou); UBC; Nanyang Technological University · 2026-06 · preprint. Six automatically built multi-agent systems fail to beat voting over five reasoning chains in 10 of 18 dataset–model pairs, at a median cost 1.7 times higher.
- [Orchestra-o1: Omnimodal Agent Orchestration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13707) (Ch. 4) — CUHK; LIGHTSPEED; Peking University; Tsinghua University; Tongji University · 2026-06 · preprint. Orchestra-o1 with GPT-5 as the lead agent scores 72.8% on OmniGAIA versus 53.9% for a single agent on the same model; for the 8B orchestrator, most of the gain comes from the scaffold, not training.
- [The Bounded Parallelizability of R&D: Theory and Application to AI](https://philiptrammell.com/static/Parallelizability.pdf) (Ch. 4) — Epoch AI; Stanford Digital Economy Lab · 2026-06 · preprint. Trammell adds a second ceiling to growth models — parallelization technology; how fast it grows determines whether an "intelligence explosion" happens and how fast it is. Theory without data.
- [When Does Multi-Agent RL Improve LLM Workflows?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24202) (Ch. 4) — Zeng, Wu, Zhang et al. (Oregon State, Penn State, Adobe, AG2AI), preprint 22 May 2026 · 2026-05 · preprint. On 0.6–4 billion-parameter models, multi-agent RL usually improves on the base, but the effect depends on workflow and scale; separate policies more often fall off a "cliff"
- [Introducing Muse Spark](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/04/introducing-muse-spark-meta-superintelligence-labs/) (Ch. 4) — Meta Superintelligence Labs, 8 Apr 2026 · 2026-04 · lab blog / report. Parallel subagents in Meta AI; the Contemplating mode scores 58% on Humanity's Last Exam and 38% on FrontierScience Research; no comparison with a single agent
- [Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460) (Ch. 4) — Stanford University · 2026-04 · preprint. At the same reasoning-token budget (500 and above), one agent is on average no worse than the best of five multi-agent architectures on the multi-hop questions of FRAMES and MuSiQue.
- [Agentic Aggregation for Parallel Scaling of Long-Horizon Agentic Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11753) (Ch. 4) — Princeton University · 2026-04 · peer-reviewed. Eight independent runs plus a trajectory "reader": for GLM-4.7-Flash on BrowseComp, 56.00% versus 27.42% for one run, but all eight runs are paid for in full.
- [Language Model Teams as Distributed Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.12229) (Ch. 4) — Princeton University; MIT; University of Cambridge; New York University · 2026-03 · preprint. Teams of 1–5 agents get a speedup from external task assignment (best cell: 3.35× in time at 1.33× tokens); when agents claim tasks themselves, the team is slower than a single agent.
- [WideSeek-R1: Exploring Width Scaling for Broad Information Seeking via Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04634) (Ch. 4) — Tsinghua University; Infinigence AI · 2026-02 · preprint. Multi-agent RL on Qwen3-4B lifts item F1 on WideSearch to 40.0% versus 28.1% for a single agent trained on the same data; the curve keeps rising up to 10 subagents.
- [Time Horizon 1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/) (Ch. 4) — METR, 29 Jan 2026 · 2026-01 · lab blog / report. The task suite grew from 170 to 228; Claude Opus 4.5's horizon is 320 minutes; doubling roughly every 89 days since 2024 (context for "one long-running agent")
- [Kimi K2.5: Visual Agentic Intelligence — Agent Swarm and PARL (+ Kimi K2.6 swarm update)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02276) (Ch. 4) — Moonshot AI · 2026-01 · preprint. Kimi K2.5: a swarm "conductor" trained with reinforcement learning reaches a target WideSearch level 3.0–4.5 times faster than a single agent; against a single agent with context cleanup, the BrowseComp gain is +3.5 pp.
- [Capable language models can outgrow the benefits of collaboration (preprint: “Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems”)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-026-01268-y) (Ch. 4) — Google Research; Google DeepMind; MIT · 2025-12 · peer-reviewed. 260 configurations under a shared token budget: the team's mean effect is 0.0% with a 95% CI of −58.7…+77.2%; the sign is set by task and architecture (from +80.8% to −70.0%).
- [Stronger-MAS: Multi-Agent RL for Collaborative LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11062) (Ch. 4) — Zhao, Hu, Wang et al. (UC San Diego, Intel), preprint 13 Oct 2025 (v5 2026) · 2025-10 · peer-reviewed. The AT-GRPO method trains a team of agents with RL: on long-horizon planning, accuracy rises from 14–47% to 96–99.5%; the ICLR 2026 venue is not confirmed
- [Chain-of-Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13167) (Ch. 4) — OPPO AI Agent Team (30 authors), preprint 6 Aug 2025 · 2025-08 · preprint. A team of agents "distilled" into a single 32B model: 55.3% on GAIA and 18.0% on HLE, according to the authors better than previous methods of the same size
- [How quick and big would a software intelligence explosion be?](https://www.forethought.org/research/how-quick-and-big-would-a-software-intelligence-explosion-be) (Ch. 4) — Tom Davidson, Tom Houlden (Forethought), 4 Aug 2025 · 2025-08 · preprint. A model of a software explosion after AI research is automated, with λ = 0.6 as the median assumption; a ~60% chance of compressing more than 3 years of progress into less than a year
- [Debate or Vote: Which Yields Better Decisions in Multi-Agent Large Language Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17536) (Ch. 4) — University of Wisconsin-Madison · 2025-08 · peer-reviewed. Voting over five independent answers without discussion is more accurate than multi-agent debate (0.769 vs 0.738 for Qwen2.5-7B); the benefit comes from the number of independent attempts, not from communication.
- [How we built our multi-agent research system (2025) and When to use multi-agent systems (2026)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system) (Ch. 4) — Anthropic · 2025-06 · lab blog / report. Anthropic: the Research system gave +90.2% over a single Opus 4 at ≈15× chat tokens; the 2026 advice: multi-agent setups use 3–10 times more tokens and buy thoroughness, not speed.
- [A multi-agent system for automating scientific discovery](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10652-y) (Ch. 4) — FutureHouse; Fordham University; University of Oxford · 2025-05 · peer-reviewed. A designed pipeline of three agents and an LLM judge, not a swarm; humans run the experiments; for dry age-related macular degeneration it proposed ripasudil (1.89× higher phagocytosis, in cell culture only); no comparison of the whole pipeline with one long-running agent at equal budget; only narrow controls (literature agents replaced by o4-mini calls; comparison with OpenAI Deep Research)
- [Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail? (MAST)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657) (Ch. 4) — Cemri, Pan, Yang et al. (UC Berkeley, Intesa Sanpaolo), NeurIPS 2025 D&B spotlight · 2025-03 · peer-reviewed.
