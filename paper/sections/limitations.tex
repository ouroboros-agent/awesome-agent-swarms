\section*{Limitations}
This report is a structured synthesis of a purposively assembled corpus. It is biased toward recent work, sources accessible to the researchers who assembled it, and systems whose developers disclose their behavior. There is no preregistered search protocol, screening flow, estimate of recall, or independent double coding.

The report reuses prior extraction and verification work for the frozen cohort; it does not re-audit every primary source of that cohort or reproduce every reported number. Numbers quoted from the later additions were re-read at their primary sources, but the additions come from targeted searches and lists, not from systematic screening up to the cutoff, so recent work outside them may be missing. Values read from plots are labeled as approximate. Source versions sometimes differ: a blog and its preprint can list different affiliations or report different parts of one study, and we cite the version that contains each quoted number.

Many controlled comparisons use small groups, short tasks, and older or smaller models. Large deployments often lack matched baselines, resource accounting, repetitions, or public traces. Model-grader outcomes can entangle task performance with grading preferences. Population simulations can be sensitive to prompts and cached policies. These limitations constrain cross-setting generalization and are the reason we avoid a pooled quantitative effect or universal scaling exponent.

The taxonomy itself requires judgment. Organizational origin can be mixed, the realized graph may be unobserved, and identity counts can be uncertain. The evidence matrix is a selected analytical mapping, not a claim that every source has complete eight-dimensional annotation.
